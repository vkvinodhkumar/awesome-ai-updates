## Terminology

- **Frontier Model**: The most advanced, state-of-the-art AI models currently available, which represent the absolute limits of computational capabilities.
- **Agentic Workflow**: A process where an AI model behaves like an independent agent—performing multi-step planning, decision-making, self-correction, and tool usage over an extended sequence without needing a human to prompt it at every step.
- **GPU Orchestration**: Software that acts as a traffic controller for specialized computer chips (GPUs), distributing work efficiently to ensure no expensive hardware sits idle.
- **Liquid Foundation Models (LFMs)**: A new type of AI model architecture designed to adapt continuously to incoming data over time. They are often more lightweight and computationally efficient than traditional Transformer models.
- **Geospatial Inference**: Using artificial intelligence to analyze geographical, climate, and satellite mapping data to draw conclusions or make environmental predictions.
- **Long-Context Inference**: The capability of an AI model to read, hold in memory, and analyze massive amounts of information (such as entire books or codebases) during a single interaction.
- **Sim-to-Real**: The technological process of training an AI or robotic system in a highly detailed virtual environment (simulation) and then successfully deploying that trained intelligence into the unpredictable physical world.
- **Data Provenance**: A documented history showing exactly where a piece of data originated, how it was handled, and who owns it, ensuring it is safe and legal to use for training AI.
