# Executive Meeting Brief

- **Key Developments:** AI is moving into "high-stakes" environments (Healthcare EHR, Legal, Cybersecurity). Automation is shifting from simple text generation to autonomous agents (Basis, Clay).
- **Risks:** Benchmark reliability (BenchMIRT) suggests that model performance may sometimes be overstated. Cybersecurity remains a primary concern for frontier models.
- **Opportunities:** Significant cost savings and speed-to-market for SMBs (ATV Big Air case). Local AI (WebGPU) offers a path to lower latency and higher privacy for customer-facing apps.
- **Recommended Actions:** 
    1. Evaluate the transition from "copilots" to "agents" for internal workflows.
    2. Review AI governance policies using the Gilbert + Tobin model as a reference.
    3. Explore local/browser-based AI deployments to reduce cloud compute costs and enhance data privacy.

## Technology Trends

1.  **Agentic Workflows:** Moving from chatbots to autonomous systems that perform multi-step tasks.
2.  **Edge/Local AI:** Utilizing WebGPU to run models on the user's device rather than the cloud.
3.  **Data Inclusivity:** Increasing focus on Global South languages and diverse datasets.
4.  **Rigorous Evaluation:** A shift toward more sophisticated benchmarks to detect data contamination and true reasoning.

## Terminology

- **ASR (Automatic Speech Recognition):** Technology that converts spoken language into text.
- **EHR (Electronic Health Record):** A digital version of a patient’s paper chart, highly regulated for privacy.
- **Frontier Model:** The most advanced class of AI models that push the boundaries of current capabilities.
- **WebGPU:** A modern technology that allows web browsers to use a computer’s graphics card to run complex tasks like AI very quickly.
- **Multi-Vector Embedding:** A way of organizing information so an AI can find very specific, related pieces of data more accurately.
- **Time Series Model:** An AI model specifically designed to predict future values based on past data collected over time (e.g., stock prices or weather).
- **Data Contamination:** When an AI model "cheats" on a test because it already saw the answers in its training data.
