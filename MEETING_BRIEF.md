# Executive Meeting Brief

### Key Developments
- **Scientific Supremacy:** AI is now tackling "Millennium Prize" level problems in mathematics and physics (Navier-Stokes).
- **Autonomous Science:** The move toward "Lab-Agents" that can run physical hardware (Quantum experiments).
- **Small Model Efficiency:** Proof that 350M parameter models can achieve high precision in structured outputs.

### Risks
- **Verification Crisis:** As AI solves complex math, the burden on human experts to verify these "proofs" increases exponentially.
- **Societal Impact:** Rapid adoption among teens outpaces our understanding of developmental risks.
- **Data Sovereignty:** The need for "Memory You Own" highlights the risks of sensitive IP being stored in centralized AI clouds.

### Opportunities
- **R&D Acceleration:** Use GPT-5.6 level agents to automate internal lab or coding workflows.
- **Cost Reduction:** Deploying smaller, fine-tuned models for structured data tasks instead of expensive API calls to large models.
- **Creative Precision:** Utilizing Images 2.5 for rapid, brand-consistent prototyping.

### Recommended Actions
1. **Pilot Agentic Workflows:** Explore the use of specialized agents (like GPT-5.6 Sol) for internal technical experiments or data calibration.
2. **Review Safety Thresholds:** Evaluate if current internal AI implementations are "over-refusing" and look into nuanced safety tuning.
3. **Audit Small Model Potential:** Investigate if 350M-1B parameter models can replace larger models for structured, repetitive data tasks to save costs.

## Technology Trends
- **Agentic Autonomy:** AI is moving from answering questions to executing multi-step physical and digital experiments.
- **Mathematical Formalization:** Increased use of "Lean" and other formal languages to verify AI-generated logic.
- **Precision Safety:** A shift from "blanket bans" on topics to "surgical refusals" of harmful content.
- **Edge Intelligence:** Focus on making tiny models perform like large models through advanced reinforcement learning (GRPO).

## Terminology

- **Navier–Stokes Problem:** A set of equations that describe the motion of fluid substances; solving it is considered one of the hardest challenges in physics.
- **Lean:** A formal proof assistant and programming language used to verify that mathematical theorems are actually correct.
- **GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization):** A reinforcement learning technique that helps models learn better decision-making by comparing groups of possible answers.
- **Qubit:** The basic unit of information in quantum computing (the quantum version of a classical "bit").
- **Multimodal-native:** An AI system built from the start to understand different types of data (like text and images) at the same time, rather than bolting them together later.
- **Structured Outputs:** AI responses that follow a strict format (like a spreadsheet or code) rather than just plain conversational text.
- **Codex:** An AI model specifically designed to understand and generate computer code.
